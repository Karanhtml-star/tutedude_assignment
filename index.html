Why Greenwood exists

About the library

Greenwood opened in 1984 as a single reading room above the old post office. Today it holds six collections and stays open seven days a week — every card is free, every visitor welcome.

A library that still believes in browsing

Greenwood was built on the idea that a library is a place first, and a collection second. The building keeps its original oak shelving and its tall south-facing windows, restored rather than replaced when the roof was rebuilt in 2009.

Every section is arranged for browsing rather than searching — spines face out, staff picks sit at eye level, and nothing is filed so precisely that a visitor can't wander into something unexpected.

The reading rooms stay quiet by design: phones are asked to stay in pockets, and the loudest sound most afternoons is the courtyard fountain through the open doors.

Membership has stayed free since the doors opened in 1984, funded by the township and a small endowment left by the library's first head librarian, Eleanor Voss.
